Where Prama sits among the products that touch data quality, and how it does the things the others do not.

About Prama Help Vendor by vendor

Five families of product touch the problem Prama addresses, and each is good at what it does. {% for c in categories %}{{ c.label }} ({{ c.examples }}){{ ', ' if not loop.last else '.' }}{% endfor %} Catalogs know what data means without checking it. Lineage tools know where it came from without measuring it. Observability tools notice that something changed, but cannot say how often they are wrong or prove anything afterwards. DQ suites check data without the owner's words. Reconciliation products agree two systems, in a product of their own.

The pattern is consistent: each answers one part of can I trust this number, and prove it? Prama keeps one record for the whole question: the meaning, declared by its owner; the controls, derived and executed at the source; the alert rate, calibrated; and the evidence, verifiable without Prama.

What the rows have in common
  • It derives what others ask somebody to type: controls from declarations, scores from evidence, and an alert threshold from a false-alarm rate.
  • It executes, not only describes. A lineage map, a glossary term or a policy becomes a control that ran, with a verdict.
  • Its evidence leaves with a verifier, so an auditor does not have to trust the platform under audit.
The practical reading

Prama is a complement, not a replacement. Keep the catalog, the lineage scanner and the reconciliation desk: Prama imports what they know, executes and evidences the controls, and publishes quality back to where people already look.
